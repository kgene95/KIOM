# Research logic audit

Evaluate the paper in both directions.

## Top-down
1. What is the stated knowledge gap?
2. What research question follows from that gap?
3. Is a hypothesis stated or implied? Is it testable by the actual design?
4. Are the primary outcomes and experiments capable of answering the question?
5. Are mechanistic aims narrower than, equal to, or broader than the measured evidence?

## Bottom-up
1. What do the actual data establish?
2. Which results are primary versus secondary or exploratory?
3. What interpretations are justified by those results?
4. Does the Discussion stay within those limits?
5. Does the Conclusion answer the original question without adding a stronger claim?

## Hypothesis integrity
- If a hypothesis is clearly prespecified, test whether the design and outcomes can support or fail to support it.
- If data existed before the hypothesis wording was created, treat the wording as exploratory/post hoc unless the user confirms prespecification.
- Do not convert a data-derived mechanistic interpretation into a historical claim about what was hypothesized before the experiment.
- Identify plausible competing explanations when they could materially change interpretation.
- If the original hypothesis is too strong for the evidence, recommend narrowing the claim rather than rewriting history.
